War ♠♥♦♣

Simulate a game of the most classic of card games.

Nicolas FLASQUE - IA Claude code

Goal

Everyone has played War as a child. Your mission: write a program that, given how the cards are initially dealt between two players, simulates the game to the end and announces who wins it.

Rules of the game

Two players face each other. Each one starts with a deck of cards held face down; the card played is always the one on top, and the cards won are put at the bottom. A deck therefore behaves like a queue (first in, first out).

1. The fight

In each round, both players reveal the top card of their deck. The higher card wins: its owner collects the cards played and slides them under their deck.

Card values, from lowest to highest (suits do not matter):

2345678910JQKA

2. War

In case of a tie, it is war! Each player first lays down 3 cards taken from the top of their deck (they stay in play but are not compared), then the game goes back to the fight step with the next card. A new tie starts a new war, and so on.

The player who finally wins collects all the cards played during the round.

3. Collecting order

The winner puts the cards back under their deck in a fixed order: first all the cards played by player 1 (in the order they were played), then all those of player 2. It does not matter which of the two won the round.

4. End of the game

  • A player wins as soon as their opponent has no cards left.
  • If, during a war, a player does not have enough cards to lay down their 3 cards or to turn over the next fight card, the game ends in a draw: PAT.
The test cases are built so that the game always ends: there is no need to detect an infinite loop.

A round in detail

Initial deal (top card on the left):

Player 1: 10D 9S 8D KH 7D 5H 6S
Player 2: 10H 7H 5C QC 2C 4H 6D
  1. 10D against 10H: tie, war!
  2. Each player lays down 3 cards: 9S 8D KH for P1, 7H 5C QC for P2.
  3. New fight: 7D against 2C. Player 1 wins.
  4. They collect the cards of P1 (10D 9S 8D KH 7D), then those of P2 (10H 7H 5C QC 2C).

Situation after this single round:

Player 1: 5H 6S 10D 9S 8D KH 7D 10H 7H 5C QC 2C
Player 2: 4H 6D

Data format

Writing a card

A card is written as its value followed by its suit, among D (diamonds), H (hearts), C (clubs) and S (spades). Examples: 4H, 8C, AS, 10D.

Input (on standard input)

Line(s)Content
1An integer N: number of cards of player 1
N linesThe cards of player 1, from the top to the bottom of the deck
1An integer M: number of cards of player 2
M linesThe cards of player 2, from the top to the bottom of the deck

Output (on standard output)

  • If a player wins: their number (1 or 2), a space, then the number of rounds played. A war, even chained with other wars, counts as one round only.
  • In case of a draw: the word PAT.

Contraintes

0 < N, M < 1000

Input / output example

Input 
3
AD
KC
QC
3
KH

Expected output 
1 3

Player 1 wins all three fights (A>K, K>Q, Q>J): player 2 has no cards left after 3 rounds.

Cases to cover

Your program will be evaluated on a variety of situations. Make sure to test:

  • a very short game (3 cards each);
  • games with a full half-deck (26 cards each), short and long;
  • a game with one war;
  • two chained wars in the same round;
  • long games (several hundred rounds);
  • games ending in PAT, in two different ways.
