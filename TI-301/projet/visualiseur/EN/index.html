3Accepted files

A tree

This is the format of saveTree, in step 4 of part 1. The first line gives the tip of the trunk, \(x\) then \(y\). Each following line describes a branch: its number, its season of appearance, the number of its parent, its direction, its length, its generation and its state (0 for a bud, 1 for wood). The branches are written in the order of a breadth-first traversal.

0.00 1.00
1 1 0 -5.35 0.76 1 1
2 1 0 41.42 1.29 1 1
3 2 1 -3.28 0.97 2 1
…

A forest

This is the format of saveForest, provided in sauver_foret.c for part 2. The first line describes the forest: FORET K E C HMAX A P_max saison. Each tree then starts with a line ARBRE id tronque nb_rameaux (tronque is 1 if the tree is truncated, 0 otherwise), followed by the tree in the format of saveTree.

FORET 24 3 72 160 0.250 20000 32
ARBRE 0 0 352
1.50 1.07
1 1 0 0.31 1.13 1 1
2 2 1 -8.97 0.75 2 1
…
ARBRE 1 0 418
…

The positions \(x\) are not brought back between 0 and \(C\): the forest wraps around when it is drawn.

4Good to know

  • Everything is computed in your browser: the file is not sent anywhere.
  • The drawing is an illustration. Only the positions of the branches come from the file. They are recomputed as readTree does, from the directions and lengths rounded to 0.01. The thickness of the branches, the maple leaves and their colours are added for the drawing: they are not part of the model.
  • The forest wraps around: a branch that leaves the image on the right reappears on the left.
  • The same file always gives exactly the same image. The drawing makes no random draw.
  • Duration: a few seconds for a tree of 5,000 branches; about fifteen seconds for a large forest (about a hundred thousand branches), GIF included. The GIF keeps about forty frames at most: for a long simulation, it shows only one season in two or three.
  • Very large files (several hundred thousand branches): choose a width of 1000 px, and untick the GIF.
